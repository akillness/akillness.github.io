## Platform policy cross-check

The video's durable half aligns with published platform policy; the excluded half conflicts with it:

- **Google people-first content guidance** (`https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content`): asks creators to assess first-hand expertise, depth, and whether readers leave satisfied; it warns against search-engine-first content produced primarily to rank.
- **Google spam policy — scaled content abuse** (`https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies`): mass-producing many pages with little added value, with or without AI, is abuse regardless of production method.
- **Google guidance on generative AI content** (`https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content`): AI can help research and structure original content, but scaled generation without added user value can violate the spam policy; accuracy, quality, relevance, and creation context remain required.
- **Naver Search Advisor's official content guidance** (`https://searchadvisor.naver.com/guide/content-basic`): recommends expertise or honest experience, a consistent site topic, source attribution, readable structure, and freshness; when AI is used, it says not to stop at generation or summary and to add the operator's experience and perspective. The page does not itself require an AI-use disclosure, so visible disclosure remains this site's stricter trust control.

## Honesty constraint for scheduled automation

This blog's nightly run cannot copy principle 5 literally: no human writes each post at 01:00. Pretending otherwise would be the exact dishonesty the policies punish. The contract therefore adapts it: AI research and drafting are disclosed in the article body, editorial judgment is owned by the evidence-gated harness (independent evidence editor plus deterministic validators), first-hand experience is never claimed, and no outcome is asserted without a dated first-party measurement.
